Investors

Every heart deserves its own record. We are building the company that makes that normal.

Fairest Heart turns a chest strap millions of people already own into an all-day, all-night heart recorder, and puts the record in the wearer’s hands in the formats clinics use. It shipped. People wear it every night. Clinicians who have seen it want their patients on it. We are raising to make it the standard way a worried person gets a record of their heart.

The problem

The record of your heart belongs to whoever can afford to make one.

Episodes are missed

Palpitations, racing, skipped beats: among the most common reasons to see a cardiologist. By the time a monitor is fitted, the episode is over.

Monitors are rationed

A clinic recorder costs hundreds of dollars for one or two days, needs a prescription and an appointment, and insurers refuse it. The maker’s own was refused after his defibrillator fired.

Consumer ECG is a snapshot

Certified consumer ECG devices record thirty seconds when you remember to. The heart does what it does at 3 a.m.

The product

Record. Mark. Share. Your heart in your hands.

A phone app and a chest strap. Every sample saved, all day and all night, with a mark and a word for how it felt. The whole record as a PDF the clinic reads and as EDF+, the format hospital software opens. No server, no account, no upload: the wearer owns the file.

Why now

Three things lined up in 2026.

The sensor is already bought

Medical-grade single-lead chest straps sell in the millions for sport. The hardware cost of entry is zero; we are software on top of a sensor people own.

Clinics want the file, not the gadget

Open formats mean the record drops into the software a clinic already runs. No integration project, no portal.

Build cost collapsed

One maker with AI tooling shipped 135,000 lines of app and 166,000 lines of tests in fifteen weeks. Capital goes to reach and trials, not headcount.

Pull

Clinicians ask for it. We did not ask them.

An electrophysiologist and a hospital cardiac imaging team in New York have seen it and asked how their patients could get it. Names and quotes follow with their written permission.

How it makes money

Three lines, one file.

Wearers

Free records. Plus $4.99 and Pro $9.99 a month keep, trend and report. Target 2,000 paying by end of 2027.

Clinics

$300 a practice a month: patients wear it before the visit, the PDF is on the desk. Target 40 practices by end of 2027.

Research

Continuous single-lead ECG with the wearer’s own marks, in open formats, with consent. Paid data access for studies.

Run-rate target end of 2027: about $280,000 to $300,000. Fixed costs under $40,000 a year.

What comes after

The analysis is built. It waits for the regulator.

The maker’s own rhythm analysis runs on every night he records. It is private by choice: the app records and does not interpret until it is cleared to. That is the second product, and the FDA pathway is the reason to raise more than one round.
