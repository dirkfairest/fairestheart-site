Why it exists

In June 2026 my defibrillator fired. The episodes that followed were too small for it to act on, and insurance refused the recorder my electrophysiologist prescribed.

So I wet a Polar H10 strap, wrote an app that saved every sample it sent, and slept with it on. Night after night. When I ended up in the emergency room on 9 August, the whole episode was on my phone, from the first odd beat to the moment the staff took over. I handed it to them as a file their software could open.

Fairest Heart is the general version of that recorder. It records, shows and shares. The analysis I built for my own heart was tuned to one heart and stays with me; what you get is the plain record, in open formats, to hand to whoever you choose.

What you get

It records, shows and shares what the strap provides. Nothing is interpreted for you.

Single-lead ECG at 130 samples per second, heart rate, R-R intervals, HRV, strap contact and motion. You see the trace and the numbers, and you decide whom to share them with.

Live ECG trace

Heart rate and HRV while the strap is on. A 5, 10 or 30 second window, the standard 25 mm/s grid.

All night, phone locked

Overnight and all-day recording in six-hour chunks. A notification keeps it alive and carries the mark button.

A mark when you feel it

One tap saves the moment. Add a diary note later: what you felt, when, and how long.

Recording viewer

Any night on a 25 mm/s × 10 mm/mV paper grid with zoom, the marks lined up, 30 seconds before each.

Your day and night

Heart rate over the day, resting heart rate, HRV, time in training zones, a sleep estimate from the strap, signal quality, Poincaré plot.

Share, in open formats

EDF+ (the format most ECG software reads), CSV, or a PDF with strips at your marks and the whole recording.

How it works

Three steps, then sleep.

Wet the strap, put it on

Any Polar H10 that streams ECG. The app finds it over Bluetooth and connects.

Recording starts on connect

Lock the phone. The night is written to the phone as EDF+ and CSV, with a copy in Downloads.

Look, mark, share

In the morning: the trace, the numbers, your marks. Share a PDF or the raw file with whoever you choose.
